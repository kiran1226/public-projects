# Decision log

| Date | Decision | Alternative | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-11-15 | Gradient boosting from scikit-learn (`HistGradientBoostingRegressor`) | LightGBM | The LightGBM wheel failed to load on macOS without a separately installed OpenMP runtime (`Library not loaded: @rpath/libomp.dylib`). The project must run after `uv sync` alone |
| 2026-11-15 | The seasonal detector takes its spread per slice from four weeks of seasonal residuals | MAD of the six reference values, with a Poisson or 1/sqrt(volume) floor | Measured on a 70-day world with one injected outage: with six points, 2.9% of session scores had p < 0.0001 and discount rate never alarmed, giving 119 incidents in two weeks and a missed outage. With the per-slice spread: 5 incidents, the outage found after one hour on the exact slice. A baseline that broken would have flattered the ML detector |
| 2026-11-15 | The boosted detector divides each residual by its slice's spread, measured on the last two weeks of the training window | Volume-based noise formula only | On the dev world a +57% jump in affiliate sessions (bot traffic) scored p = 0.002 and the slice did not alarm: large, noisy slices in the same calibration table set the bar. After the change the bot incident is found in one hour on the exact slice |
| 2026-11-15 | The boosted detector ranks planned-event hours against earlier planned-event hours, and keeps them out of the normal calibration table | One table for everything; or planned hours excluded from calibration and judged against normal hours | One table: a newsletter in the calibration window hid a real incident a week later. Excluded and judged against normal hours: about 50 alerts per promotion. The separate table is mostly training data, so it is somewhat optimistic; that is stated in the code |
| 2026-11-15 | Across metrics, alarms link only when they are the same slice on the dimensions both metrics share | Containment across metrics | On the dev world a coupon error in the Netherlands was chained into a card outage in France through discount:email and conversion:FR, email, and reported as missed |
| 2026-11-15 | Merged incidents stay on record, and the evaluation scores every alert as raised | Delete the younger incident when two merge | Found in review: on the dev world the boosted detector raised 24 alerts and 19 survived, so false incidents were reported as 4 where 9 had been raised. On another seed a correct alert was merged away and its incident reported as missed |
| 2026-11-15 | An incident member links new alarms only within three hours of its own last alarm | Link to any member the incident ever had | Found in review: one alert kept alive by a single slice adopted everything. That, not the detector, produced the static baseline's 0 of 14 |
| 2026-11-15 | Beyond a table's 99th percentile, p-values come from an exponential tail through its 95th and 99th percentiles | Ranks only | Found in review: a rank cannot go below 1 / (n + 1), so with a planned-event table of a few thousand scores one slice could never alarm alone, and one unconfirmed anomaly masked a calibration table for four weeks. P-values under 0.01 now depend on the tail's shape |
| 2026-11-15 | Time to detect counts the hour an alert needs to be complete | Opening tick minus onset tick | Found in review: an alert on the 14:00 hour is raised at 15:00. The report understated every detection by one hour |
| 2026-11-15 | The scored planned events differ from the warm-up ones, and one incident sits inside a promotion | Scored events that copy the warm-up events | Found in review: "quiet on planned events" was measured on replicas of what the detector had trained on, and nothing tested an incident during an event |
| 2026-11-15 | The report can show the same scenarios on other seeds | One seed | Found in review: the committed seed was the only one of four on which the boosted detector beat the seasonal one on recall. What held on every seed was fewer false incidents at similar recall |
| 2026-11-15 | Alarms are linked by slice containment, and the overall series never links two alarms | Link alarms whose dimension values do not conflict | The looser rule links a country alarm to a platform alarm, and through the overall series to everything, so simultaneous incidents collapse into one |
| 2026-11-16 | Alarms of a metric are explained by one another before they are grouped, and an open incident takes new alarms only through its roots | Link alarms whenever one slice contains the other | On the dev world a bot-traffic incident that started inside a planned promotion was never raised by any detector: the promotion's alert held the total, the total contains every slice, so the alert adopted the bot alarms. A slice is now a thing of its own unless a narrower slice accounts for it or the rest of a broader slice moved with it |
| 2026-11-16 | A slice is separate from its broader slice only when the broader slice moved less than a third as much outside it and the unexplained part is at least two alarm levels | Either test alone | Unexplained strength alone: 903 false incidents with the seasonal detector, because in a large event every slice is far out of band and none matches its parent to the percent. Share alone splits small slices on noise. The two values were picked from a grid on the dev world |
| 2026-11-16 | Each slice is compared with each broader slice, outside itself | Predict the slice from all its broader slices together (main effects) and compare | The combined prediction assumes the cause is spread over every dimension. A coupon error in one category sits in one parent only, and its sub-slices were reported as separate incidents |
| 2026-11-16 | Roots that moved the other way are taken out of a slice before it is judged | Judge every slice on its raw deviation | Bot traffic pulled total conversion down during a promotion that lifted it. The total then looked flat, and each country's rise was reported as an incident of its own |
| 2026-11-16 | Evidence is read over four ticks, the longest window a detector tests, and the replay refuses a detector that tests a longer one | Three ticks | The four-hour test goes on firing for two hours after an incident ends. Read over three ticks those hours looked normal, nothing explained the alarm, and about 20 one-tick incidents were raised |
| 2026-11-16 | Two simultaneous incidents on overlapping slices of different dimensions may be reported as one; stated as a limit | Take the shared slice out of both and judge each on the rest | Tried: duplicate alerts on the dev world went from 4 to between 24 and 33, because every large event has slices that overlap |
| 2026-11-16 | A slice explained only by several roots together belongs to each of their parts | It belongs to the largest root's part only | The larger dev promotion lifts Germany, Spain and France well above the other countries. From hour to hour the grouper read it as one root (everything) or three (the countries), and each flip opened new alerts for two of the three: 10 alerts on one promotion, 6 after the change |
| 2026-11-16 | A weakly out-of-band root stays with the incident it was just alarming in | Only an incident whose roots have all gone quiet is continued | At the end of an incident the broad slices fall back into their bands before the narrow ones, which then have nothing to be explained by. Over four seeds duplicate alerts went from 17 to 8 with no incident lost. A root at least two alarm levels out on its own is never taken this way, so an outage inside a promotion is still raised |
| 2026-11-16 | The boosted detector also tests each hour together with the three before it | Hourly test only | Two dev incidents (a slow provider ramp and an unlogged conversion drop) stay at 3 to 4 standard deviations an hour, under the bar for one slice among 700, for as long as they last. Hourly only, the detector missed one of them on the dev world; with the second test it finds both in about five hours |
| 2026-11-16 | A four-hour pass counts only if the window before it passed as well | Treat the four-hour test like the hourly one: Bonferroni and two passes in three ticks; or give it a smaller share of the level | Two windows in a row share three hours, so persistence filtered nothing and the second test brought 19 extra false incidents on the dev world. Measured over four seeds (56 incidents, 48 weeks): as before 56 found, 126 false; held on two windows 56 found, 92 false; a fifth of the level instead 55 found, 92 false; held two hours apart 55 found, 76 false. Holding on consecutive windows is the only one that lost no incident. It costs the slow incidents up to an hour |
| 2026-11-16 | The seasonal detector stays hourly | Give it the same four-hour test | Its p-value assumes normal residuals. On quiet weeks 0.8% to 1.7% of payment-success slice-hours had p < 0.0001 where 0.01% are expected, and it raised 185 false alerts. The forecaster ranks against calibration data and does not have the problem |
| 2026-11-16 | Benjamini-Hochberg per metric, at q divided by the number of metrics | One family across all 706 series | During a promotion, hundreds of real discoveries on discount rate raised the rank that noise on payment success had to beat, and unrelated false alerts appeared for as long as the promotion ran |
| 2026-11-16 | The static baseline's thresholds are tuned on the warm-up weeks to fire on one slice-hour in a thousand | Hand-picked 40%, 30% and 10% | Hand-picked thresholds fired constantly; a baseline that bad would make any detector look good, and no team would leave it switched on. Tuned, and with the strength bug below fixed, it finds 7 of 14 dev incidents with 31 false ones in twelve weeks. Moving the threshold never gets both: three times looser, 9 of 14 with 78; three times stricter, 4 with 18; ten times stricter, 2 with 10 |
| 2026-11-16 | A slice-hour is scored only when its source table has every row the slice is made of | Freshness per table: scored unless the table has no rows at all for the hour | Found in review: a table late for one country read as that country falling to zero |
| 2026-11-16 | A late-data label is matched only by alerts on metrics built from the late table | Any alert in the window on a compatible slice | The label says "no alert because of the missing data". An unrelated alert in the same hours on another table is a false incident of its own kind and is now counted as one |
| 2026-11-16 | An incident id is built from the world, the opening tick and the alarms that opened it | World, metric, slice, opening tick | Found in review: two groups opened in the same tick could be named after the same slice and get the same id |
| 2026-11-16 | An incident keeps the name it was raised with; its current roots are stored beside it | Rename as the picture sharpens | Someone may already be acting on the alert. A name that changes under them is worse than a first name that is one level too broad |
| 2026-11-16 | `revwatch eval` writes `docs/eval-report.md` only for a full run on a named world | Always write to the default path | Found in review: `--detector seasonal` replaced the committed report with a partial one |
| 2026-11-16 | In the four-hour sum one hour counts for at most four of its slice's spreads | Sum the residuals as they are | One badly forecast hour kept four windows in a row significant and so passed persistence by itself. Four seeds, every incident still found: no cap 84 false incidents, cap six 78, five 72, four 57. At three, 53 of 56 incidents are found |
| 2026-11-16 | Every detector reports strength in units of its 95% band | The static baseline divided by its alarm threshold | Found in review: grouping reads 1 as the edge of the band. With the baseline's scale most of its alarms looked like they were inside the band and were split into separate alerts. Together with the next row, about 450 false incidents per world became about 30. Earlier baseline figures in this log were inflated by this bug |
| 2026-11-16 | The static baseline skips a reference hour that is a confirmed incident and uses the week before | Compare with last week regardless | Found in review: each incident it found came back a week later as a false one, which broke the detector contract and handicapped the baseline |
| 2026-11-16 | Slices are judged twice per tick, the second time with the roots of the first known | One pass, narrowest first | Found in review: Germany down and France on Android down at once became one incident, because the narrow slice was compared with a total that still held Germany's fall |
| 2026-11-16 | A slice still out of band by itself beside the narrower root that dominates it gives an open incident no claim; nor does a lapsed root carried by a broader new one | Any carried slice that alarms is a handle | Found in review: an open alert on a mild promotion took an outage five times the size of its slice, and an old narrow alert took a new promotion |
| 2026-11-16 | A root is current while it explains alarms, whether or not it alarms itself | Recency from the root's own last alarm | Found in review: a root that never passed false-alarm control never aged, so the same hours gave one alert or two depending on whether it had alarmed once |
| 2026-11-16 | An alert counts for a scenario only on a metric the scenario moves, in that direction | Window and related slice | Found in review: an alert named after the overall series counted for any scenario in its window. | 2026-11-16 | Scenario times must be on the hour; an incident needs an effect; a ramp cannot be negative; late data takes no slice | Accept and round | Found in review: 10:30 to 10:45 injected nothing and was labelled as an incident |
| 2026-11-17 | The scripted investigator only blames kinds of change that can move the alert's metric in the alert's direction | Blame the nearest earlier entry whose scope fits | Measured on the dev world: the naive version proposed rolling back a release for a rise in sessions, twice, with high confidence and evidence that recomputes. | 2026-11-17 | `CAUSE_NOT_SUPPORTED`: a proposal that blames no change is withheld unless a verified citation shows the alert's metric moving in the alert's direction in a row about the target.
